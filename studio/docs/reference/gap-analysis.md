# Cuby Studio Variant A · reference gap analysis

27 November 2026 · Phone-first walkthrough and house configurator

**Lead with authored lighting and staging.** Cuby already has the essential walkthrough and configurator mechanics. Its largest visual gaps are incomplete and out-of-date indirect lighting, a single living-room reflection capture reused throughout the house, and sparse room and site dressing. Shapespark shows that detailed static global illumination, room-local reflections and carefully authored scenes are achievable on a phone with browser rasterization. Arcware establishes a separate benchmark for streaming an Unreal configurator. Its image quality is unavailable in this environment.

## Scope and evidence

- The Shapespark homepage opens AJAX portfolio fragments that contain the scene iframes with `#autoplay&help`. - Headless Chromium ran with SwiftShader at 1280 × 800, DPR 1. The Shapespark CDN intermittently failed through Chromium's proxy connection. The capture routed the same CDN GETs through curl and fulfilled the browser requests with their decoded bodies. Wire figures below use curl's encoded GET body sizes, excluding HTTP headers and telemetry. Start and stop times, shader compilation, and software frame rate are unsuitable for device comparisons.
- Cuby was built from this checkout with Vite and previewed on port 4182. The comparisons use the phone asset tier at DPR 1, with `frames=4` for the software-GPU capture. `tools/room-shots.mjs` supplied the eye-height interiors and `tools/shot.mjs` supplied the authored exterior camera. Cuby ordinarily allows 24 still samples on this tier. The rooms, lighting setups and cameras differ across products.
- The Arcware browser shell connects to the signalling service and reaches its stream-opening overlay. Chromium's video element remained at 0 × 0 with `readyState=0`. The page contains a video container and toast, plus the Arcware SDK and a small bridge. The bridge validates six hyphen-delimited integers, sends `{ LoadConfig: "1-1-1-1-1-1" }` to Unreal at 5, 8, 12, 18 and 25 seconds, and builds a clipboard share URL from a matching Unreal response. There are zero browser-side category or option mappings in that bridge. Each field's meaning, index base, range and selected finish are unknown. The visible SDK options concern video, sound, fullscreen, connection and input, including touch and gamepad settings. The Unreal configurator controls and render settings remain unverified. ## Shapespark rendering audit

The three scene manifests are version 1.5.0, served by the same v1869 player. The browser’s run reported WebGL 2 via ANGLE/SwiftShader. The bundle has additional paths behind developer flags. Their presence establishes implementation, with activation assessed from the normal configuration.

- Geometry uses nine scene-specific `.buf` files. Vertices, encoded normals, 16/32-bit index buffers, mesh tables, transforms, bounds and separate material and lightmap UVs are transferred independently. Some files are empty when their index width is unused. The inspected vertex response advertises HTTP Brotli. The classic pack path and its WebAssembly helpers are distinct from the bundle's newer segmented Meshopt path. The trace contains no standalone glTF/GLB geometry, Draco payloads, KTX2 or KTX1 material transfers. [Vertex response](https://cdn0.shapespark.com/showcase/loft_apartment_by_motionwave/v1/vertices.buf), [viewer JavaScript](https://cdn0.shapespark.com/webwalk/v1869/walk.min.js)
- The measured Basis texture resolution is the level-zero source size in each file header. Standard image branches include JPG and PNG for textures and WebP or PNG for legacy lightmaps. The JPG cover images are 1,920 × 1,080. The CDN also serves the viewer, GLSL, WebAssembly geometry helper and Basis transcoder. Basis can shrink transfer and GPU residency when it transcodes into a supported compressed target. [Basis file format](https://github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal/wiki/.basis-File-Format-and-ETC1S-Texture-Video-Specification)
- RGBM stores RGB plus a multiplier to recover HDR. The shipped lightmaps above use that packing. The shared viewer also implements YCoCgM and RGB10A2 paths. No independent reflection cubemap or diffuse probe-grid transfers appeared. The manifests carry locations, per-object assignments and box bounds. The runtime builds/refilters room reflections using scene data. Raw HDR or EXR files did not transfer. [Viewer shaders](https://cdn0.shapespark.com/webwalk/v1869/lib/shaders-glsl.js)

A separate cold Cedar Ridge day load on Cuby’s low tier recorded 11.85 MB in same-origin response bodies by readiness, including 6.14 MB model JSON, 1.15 MB model images, 1.71 MB tiling maps, 1.02 MB lightmap and 0.44 MB sky. The browser confirmed the 1,024² atlas was bound and reported zero skipped models. This is the local preview’s HTTP and cache behavior. The preview reported Three.js texture-initialization warnings during loading, although the review renders completed.

The emulated iPhone 13 run of Loft retained the 4,096 px lightmap and shared atlases while reducing material transfer from 27.39 MB to 14.56 MB. Its complete observed total was 36.62 MB. The view list and bottom controls remained accessible in portrait, while the vertical composition cropped the sides of the room. Chromium touch emulation covers layout and asset selection. Device sensors, real phone GPU performance and Safari behavior remain unmeasured.

## Visual comparisons

Reference is on the left. Cuby is on the right, running Cedar Ridge in the Day preset on the low tier. The sheets are reduced from 1,280 × 800 live browser captures. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite returned one visual review for each pair. Its observations are summarized with the independent review below.

### 1. Living room scale and light

![Motion Wave Loft View 1 next to the Cuby Cedar Ridge great room](../../.cache/ref/shapespark/pair-01-living.jpg) (local only: third-party screenshots are not committed)

Loft balances the skylight, bright ledges and darker recesses. Upholstery, shelving and small objects give the seating area depth. Cuby has legible wood flooring and a direct sun patch. Its sparse media wall and the abrupt difference between the sun patch and the subdued wall gradients make the composition feel less lived-in. **Gemini** flagged weak corner shading and a soft ceiling surface. It credited the wood floor and foreground daylight.

### 2. Kitchen materials and practical lighting

![Velux kitchen and dining next to the Cuby Cedar Ridge kitchen](../../.cache/ref/shapespark/pair-02-kitchen.jpg) (local only: third-party screenshots are not committed)

Velux separates veined stone, wood, glossy metal and matte surfaces under an authored layer of ceiling and pendant lighting. Cuby's island, hardware and fitted cabinets provide a clear layout. Its countertop and panel tones are closer together, the window view washes out, and the light below the base cabinets reads as a bright strip. **Gemini** credited the clean island geometry and called out surface definition and the sparse ceiling.

### 3. Bedroom staging and bake drift

![Velux master bedroom next to the Cuby Cedar Ridge primary suite](../../.cache/ref/shapespark/pair-03-bedroom.jpg) (local only: third-party screenshots are not committed)

Velux gives the room focal depth through skirting, panel lines, layered bedding and deliberately placed decor. Cuby shows clear directional window light, but there is a conspicuous rectangular dark imprint across the bare left wall and floor. The Cedar day atlas was last updated in `28baae1`, before the furnishing changes in `1628ea3`. The current atlas still binds because the signature does not include loose occluders. The mark is consistent with old furniture shadowing. Confirm against a fresh bake before changing exposure or AO. **Gemini** credited the floor sunlight and flagged the dark rectangle, which it mistakenly described as a fireplace.

### 4. Exterior composition and surrounding detail

![Gratio at street level next to the Cuby Cedar Ridge exterior](../../.cache/ref/shapespark/pair-04-exterior.jpg) (local only: third-party screenshots are not committed)

Gratio uses a continuous foreground of paved edges, street objects at recognizable scale and adjacent massing. Cedar has a convincing PNW silhouette, varied timber and metal, a photographic sky and varied planting around the terrace. The dark glazing, heavily shaded facade and large coarse foreground reduce facade readability in this view. The tower and detached house serve different design briefs. The comparison is about composition, material readability and lighting on the approach. **Gemini** flagged foreground occlusion, muddy ground and weak wood-panel light, and liked the rock and grass variation.

**Phone ceiling.** Fixed geometry and a few authored lighting states can approach Shapespark's static presentation with offline GI, selected material detail, and scene-local environment probes. Plan editing, object swaps and continuous sun/sky changes invalidate parts of that precomputation. Local WebGL must fit its GPU work, decoded textures and thermal budget on the handset. Accurate view-dependent mirror reflections, fully dynamic multi-bounce GI and high-detail streamed Unreal rendering are outside the practical baseline for those same phones. Arcware moves rendering off the phone and trades local GPU load for session startup, bandwidth, latency and server capacity.
